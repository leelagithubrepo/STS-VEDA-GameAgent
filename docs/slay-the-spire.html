Implementation one

Slay the Spire

A single-player deck-building roguelike and the first game used to validate VEDA’s observation, decision, prediction, and learning architecture.

01

Why this game fits VEDA

A run climbs a branching map through fights, Elites, events, merchants, rest sites, treasure rooms, and bosses. Each decision changes a persistent state: deck, relics, potions, HP, gold, and route. Enemy intent is visible before the player acts, making checked prediction both possible and useful.

Combat

Cards use Energy. VEDA checks the hand, targets, enemy intent, Block, debuffs, and end-turn effects before advising.

Deck

Card rewards, removals, upgrades, draw, scaling, and consistency change the run’s future decision space.

Resources

Relics persist, potions are consumable, and HP and gold make routes and merchants meaningful decisions.

Risk

Elites and bosses offer outsized rewards but require matchup-aware preparation, not a fixed route.

04

Slay-the-Spire-specific learning

Safety discipline

Current screen over memory; legality before line; exact enemy intent, Block, and end-turn damage before a survival claim; and every claimed lethal checked against visible HP.

Evidence gate

A successful combat can produce a proposed lesson. It changes VEDA’s strategy only after review, supporting evidence, implementation, and a regression test.
