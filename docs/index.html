1. What is VEDA?

VEDA is an autonomous-game-agent project. Before playing a new game, she researches how it works, stores attributable knowledge, studies decisions and outcomes, then learns from her own play. Slay the Spire is her first target; the underlying design is intended to transfer to other strategy games.

3. VEDA architecture

Observe → Understand → Retrieve knowledge → Legal choices → Reason → Decide → Act → Verify → Learn

Knowledge

Source-linked facts, strategic principles, situational advice, and hypotheses remain distinct.

Safety gates

Fresh-state checks, card/energy legality, target validation, combat arithmetic, survival forecasts, and result comparison.

Input verification

A controller press is an attempt, not success. The next screenshot must confirm the expected transition before VEDA continues.

Experience

Decision records retain state, choices, prediction, result, and eventual outcome. A single result never becomes a rule.

What VEDA has automated so far

Screen-to-state discipline

Each new screenshot replaces remembered state. HP, energy, enemy HP, intent, hand, map node, and reward screen are re-read before the next recommendation.

Combat preflight

Recommendations check visible cards, energy, explicit targets, Block, Weak, Vulnerable, Artifact, and—when fully observed—survival after enemy intent.

Controller verification

A PS controller instruction is only an attempted action. VEDA confirms it, flags an unexpected transition, or asks for a fresh screen rather than silently advancing.

Potion safety

Full-inventory swaps require the named potion gained, named potion discarded, confirmed capacity, and a current ledger. Icons alone are never enough.

Route and boss safety

Map symbols and boss identity are not assumed from memory. Ambiguous nodes are treated as uncertain until selection and encounter evidence agree.

Local learning evidence

Each verified decision can retain its state, predicted result, observed result, and outcome without automatically turning one run into a strategic rule.

4. VEDA vs. LLM Ongoing

VEDA owns

Structured knowledge and provenance; game-state ledger; action legality; arithmetic checks; predictions; verification; and durable experience records.

LLM currently assists

Live screenshot interpretation, ambiguous UI reading, and flexible reasoning where the local system is not yet calibrated.

Direction of travel

Move deterministic state, inventory, turn math, and verification into VEDA first. Retain the LLM as an auditor for uncertainty—not as hidden game logic.

6. Learnings so far

  • Focused decks outperform indiscriminate card collection.
  • Live screenshots are authoritative; plans must yield to observed state.
  • Potion identity and inventory must be tracked, not guessed from icons.
  • Every proposed turn needs legal cards, energy math, targets, and a predicted result. Prediction telemetry scores only explicitly checked fields.
  • Controller inputs and potion icons are not proof: the next screen and named inventory are authoritative.
  • A successful run is evidence—not a universal strategic rule.
  • One missed potion target can invalidate a survival forecast. Recovery must begin with the next observed intent, not the original plan.
  • Boss identification must wait for readable encounter evidence; Bronze Automaton's map icon alone was not enough.
  • Prediction quality should be reported as checked outcomes and coverage, not as an unearned confidence percentage.
