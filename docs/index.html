Research-first game-playing agent

VEDA

An agent learning Slay the Spire through research, structured knowledge, observed play, verification, and experience—not a fixed human strategy.

1. What is VEDA?

VEDA is an autonomous-game-agent project. Before playing a new game, she researches how it works, stores attributable knowledge, studies decisions and outcomes, then learns from her own play. Slay the Spire is her first target; the underlying design is intended to transfer to other strategy games.

2. Current level Ongoing

Play mode

Ironclad, Ascension 0. A human currently executes PS5 inputs while VEDA provides verified, one-step recommendations.

Progress

Act 1 completed. The Slime Boss was defeated with 79/80 HP remaining.

Autonomy boundary

Local vision is observation-only until it passes real QuickTime-frame calibration. Unknown or low-confidence states fail closed.

3. VEDA architecture

Observe → Understand → Retrieve knowledge → Legal choices → Reason → Decide → Act → Verify → Learn

Knowledge

Source-linked facts, strategic principles, situational advice, and hypotheses remain distinct.

Safety gates

Fresh-state checks, card/energy legality, target validation, combat arithmetic, survival forecasts, and result comparison.

Experience

Decision records retain state, choices, prediction, result, and eventual outcome. A single result never becomes a rule.

4. VEDA vs. LLM Ongoing

VEDA owns

Structured knowledge and provenance; game-state ledger; action legality; arithmetic checks; predictions; verification; and durable experience records.

LLM currently assists

Live screenshot interpretation, ambiguous UI reading, and flexible reasoning where the local system is not yet calibrated.

Direction of travel

Move deterministic state, inventory, turn math, and verification into VEDA first. Retain the LLM as an auditor for uncertainty—not as hidden game logic.

6. Learnings so far

  • Focused decks outperform indiscriminate card collection.
  • Live screenshots are authoritative; plans must yield to observed state.
  • Potion identity and inventory must be tracked, not guessed from icons.
  • Every proposed turn needs legal cards, energy math, targets, and a predicted result.
  • A successful run is evidence—not a universal strategic rule.
